Steam Guard codes in Jace Passwords

Jace Passwords can show and fill Steam's 5-character sign-in codes (like 7KQ2F), right next to your Steam password. To do that it needs your Steam Guard secret, the key that makes the codes. Steam doesn't show it in its own app, so pick the way that fits you:

Before you start: setting up a new authenticator for Steam replaces the one in the Steam app on your phone (only one can be active). Steam may hold trades and Community Market listings for a few days after you change it. Write down the revocation code (it starts with R) that you're shown: it's the only way back in if you lose the secret.

1. You already have the secret quickest

If another app already makes your Steam codes, you can move them over without touching Steam:

  1. Bitwarden or KeePassXC: copy the 2FA field. It looks like steam://ABCD….
  2. Aegis: export (unencrypted) and copy the Steam entry's link: otpauth://steam/….
  3. KeePass with KeeTrayTOTP: copy the link with encoder=steam.
  4. Steam Desktop Authenticator or steamguard-cli: open your .maFile and copy all of it (see below).

Then jump to add it to Jace Passwords.

2. On Windows: Steam Desktop Authenticator

A free, open-source app that becomes your Steam authenticator and saves the secret in a file.

  1. Download it from github.com/Jessecar96/SteamDesktopAuthenticator and unzip it into a folder of its own.
  2. If the Steam app on your phone is your authenticator now, remove it first: Steam app → Steam Guard → Remove Authenticator. (You'll get codes by email in the meantime.)
  3. Open Steam Desktop Authenticator and choose Setup New Account. Sign in to Steam.
  4. Follow its steps: confirm your phone number and the email Steam sends you.
  5. Write down the revocation code it shows (R12345). Keep it somewhere safe, not just on this computer.
  6. When it asks for an encryption passkey, leave it empty for now (you can add one after), so the next step works.
  7. Open the maFiles folder next to the app. Open the file named like 7656119….maFile with Notepad, select all and copy.

3. On Linux or macOS: steamguard-cli

The same idea in a terminal: github.com/dyc3/steamguard-cli.

  1. Install it: on Arch from the AUR (steamguard-cli); otherwise download a release or follow the install steps on its page.
  2. If the Steam app on your phone is your authenticator now, remove it first: Steam app → Steam Guard → Remove Authenticator.
  3. Run steamguard setup and sign in. Confirm your phone number and the email Steam sends you.
  4. Write down the revocation code it prints (R12345).
  5. Don't set an encryption passkey yet (just press Enter), so the file stays readable.
  6. Open ~/.config/steamguard-cli/maFiles/ and copy everything in your .maFile, for example: cat ~/.config/steamguard-cli/maFiles/*.maFile

4. Add it to Jace Passwords

  1. Open Jace Passwords (desktop app, web app, Android or iPhone) and find your Steam login, or make one with your Steam username and password and the website store.steampowered.com.
  2. Tap Edit.
  3. Paste what you copied into the Two-factor code (2FA) box: the whole .maFile, a steam:// secret, or an otpauth://steam/ link.
  4. Under the box it should say Steam Guard codes (5 characters). Save.
  5. Check the code Jace Passwords shows matches the one in Steam Desktop Authenticator or steamguard. It changes every 30 seconds.
  6. From now on, Jace Passwords fills the Steam Guard code after your password when you sign in to Steam in your browser, and shows it in the apps to copy.

Good to know

  1. Trade and market confirmations aren't in Jace Passwords: it makes sign-in codes only. Keep Steam Desktop Authenticator or steamguard-cli for confirming trades.
  2. Keep the .maFile safe or delete it: anyone with it can make your codes. Once your codes work in Jace Passwords (where your vault is encrypted), either set an encryption passkey in that app or delete the file if you don't need confirmations.
  3. Lost everything? Use your revocation code at store.steampowered.com/twofactor/manage or Steam Support to remove the authenticator.
  4. Want the Steam app back as your authenticator? Setting it up again in the Steam app replaces this one, and the codes in Jace Passwords stop working.
